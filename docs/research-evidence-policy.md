# Research evidence policy

The explorer is a workspace for judgement. It is not a ranking, a novelty detector, or a citation manager.

## What a status means

A solid circle stroke means the node's claim has been checked at the level its status names: verified, abstract checked, or metadata checked. A dashed stroke means unverified, hypothesis, or needs literature. A dotted, faded stroke means superseded. A thesis-problem circle is labelled with the research problem. Under it, each research stage is one circle labelled with that stage. Opening a stage shows that stage's records, labelled with their titles. The status words and the rest of the record are in the popup. Colour and line style are not the only signal.

A publication's verification status is separate from the node's. A link can say what a source is being asked to support, and still be unverified. `verified_full_text` on an evidence link requires a note. The seeded literature does not use that status.

## What the app will not do

- It will not invent a DOI, venue, page range, or author.
- It will not turn a planning label such as "medium feasibility" into a measured score. The inspector and the comparison both call that a planning assessment.
- It will not declare a topic novel, and it will not sort candidates by readiness. Readiness is a checklist.
- It will not hide a gap that has no source. It says the evidence is still being reviewed.
- It will not include superseded records in search unless that option is on. They stay visible on the canvas, with the dotted border, so a later reader can see what was set aside.

## How to write a gap

Prefer a bounded sentence: what the reviewed studies do not yet establish, or what is still a hypothesis. Do not write "no one has studied this" unless that survey has actually been done and recorded.

## Comparison

Comparison lines up the same fields and writes "Not specified" where a field is empty. Counts of verification statuses, when shown, are described as counts and not a ranking.
